The Theory

1D Random Walk

The starting point is the simplest possible random process: a walker on the integers, beginning at the origin, taking a step of $\pm 1$ at each tick with equal probability. No boundaries, no bias — just the accumulation of independent coin flips. Everything on this page builds from that one rule.

The Walk

A single walk of $N$ steps produces one trajectory $X_0, X_1, \dots, X_N$, where $X_n = X_{n-1} + \xi_n$ and each $\xi_n \in \{-1, +1\}$ is drawn independently with $P(\xi_n = +1) = P(\xi_n = -1) = \tfrac{1}{2}$. That trajectory is the fundamental object — everything else here is a statistic computed over many of them.

A single walk's trajectory, position vs. step.

Trial: An Ensemble of Walks

Run the same experiment $M$ times — $M$ independent walks, all of length $N$, all starting at the origin — and look at where each one ends up. This ensemble is a trial, and its observable is the net displacement $X_N$ of each walk. Two things fall out empirically almost immediately:

The expected net displacement is zero, $\langle X_N \rangle \approx 0$ — the walk is unbiased, so left and right steps cancel on average. And the spread around that zero grows with the square root of the step count,

$$\sigma_N \approx \sqrt{N}.$$

As $M$ grows, the distribution of net displacements across the ensemble converges toward a Gaussian — the Central Limit Theorem showing up directly in simulation, not just in the derivation.

List: Scaling with N

Repeat the trial at increasing step counts $N_1 < N_2 < \cdots$ and track how $\sigma_N$ moves. Plotting $\sigma_N$ against $N$ confirms the $\sqrt{N}$ scaling directly: variance grows linearly with the number of steps, so the standard deviation grows with its square root. This is the same diffusive scaling that shows up everywhere from Brownian motion to random-walk models of diffusion.

What's Next

This walk is unbounded — the walker can drift arbitrarily far in either direction, and there's no equilibrium to speak of. The next chapter, Boundary Conditions, confines the walk to a finite interval with reflecting boundaries. That single change turns the walk from a diffusion process into a thermalizing one — the $\sqrt{N}$ scaling above gives way to a logarithmic entropy growth toward equilibrium.
