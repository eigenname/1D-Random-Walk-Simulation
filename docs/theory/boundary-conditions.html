The Theory

Boundary Conditions

Confine the unbounded walk to a finite interval $[a, b]$ with reflecting boundaries, and the whole character of the process changes — from a diffusion that spreads forever to one that settles into equilibrium. This is the model behind BoundWalk.

Reflecting Boundaries

Rather than absorbing the walker at the edges, a reflecting (Neumann) boundary folds any overshoot back into the domain: a step that would land past $b$ bounces back by exactly the amount it overshot, and likewise at $a$. The fold-back rule is applied repeatedly so it holds even for step sizes much larger than the domain itself — the walker always ends the step somewhere inside $[a, b]$.

Thermalization

Each step here is drawn independently of the last — the walk has no memory of its direction or history, which places it in the overdamped Langevin limit: the statistical description of a particle that has already fully thermalized with its surroundings. Run long enough, and the empirical position distribution converges to uniform over $[a, b]$ regardless of where the walk started — the system "forgets" its initial condition, the same way a gas forgets which corner of a box it was released into.

A bounded trajectory with reflections, alongside its position distribution converging toward uniform.

Entropy and KL Divergence

Two information-theoretic observables track that convergence directly. The Shannon entropy of the empirical position distribution at step $n$,

$$H[X_n] = -\sum_i p_i \ln p_i,$$

grows from zero (the walker's exact starting position, a delta function) toward the Boltzmann supremum $\ln W$, where $W$ is the number of accessible position bins — the maximum entropy state is the uniform distribution. In parallel, the KL divergence from the uniform reference distribution,

$$D_{KL}(P \,\|\, U) = \sum_i p_i \ln \frac{p_i}{u_i},$$

starts large and decays toward zero as the empirical distribution $P$ approaches the uniform $U$ — a direct measure of how far from equilibrium the walk still is.

Entropy Scaling Law

Sweep over step count $N$ the same way as the unbounded case, and the maximal entropy reached by each run, $S_{max}$, grows logarithmically rather than the $\sqrt{N}$ behavior seen without boundaries:

$$S_{max} \sim \ln N.$$

This connects directly to Boltzmann's entropy formula $S = k_B \ln W$: as $N$ grows, the walk explores more of its bounded domain, $W$ grows with it, and the entropy ceiling rises logarithmically. The standard deviation of position becomes logarithmic in $N$ as well, in contrast to the $\sqrt{N}$ scaling of the free walk.
